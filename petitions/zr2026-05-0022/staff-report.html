Request

A request at 412 E. Wylie Street for Conditional Use approval to allow the use "Dwelling, Duplex" in the Residential Small Lot (R3) zoning district, by Kevin Spicer. The petitioner also requests variances from the maximum vehicle parking allowance and from the vehicle parking location and design standards.

Report

The property is zoned Residential Small Lot (R3) and is approximately 0.23 acres in size. All surrounding properties are also zoned R3 and contain single-family homes. The subject property currently contains a one-story single-family home and is located within the Bryan Park neighborhood. It is not designated as a historic site and is not subject to historic regulations. An unbuilt, 36'-wide platted right-of-way for S. Palmer Avenue runs along the west property line, and a 12' platted, unimproved alley runs along the south property line. On-street parking is allowed along both sides of E. Wylie Street from Dunn Street to Washington Street.

This petition was first heard at the June 25, 2026 BZA meeting and was continued to give the petitioner an opportunity to address comments raised at that hearing. Since then, the petitioner has revised the proposed elevations and modified the proposed access and parking arrangement. The revised elevations give the building a one-story appearance from E. Wylie Street, while the roofline orientation still matches the block face and runs east/west.

The petitioner has also modified the parking arrangement to remove the previously proposed drivecut on E. Wylie Street. The revised site plan instead shows a new parking area off the alley on the south side of the property, accessed via a portion of the alley to be improved from the existing stub of S. Palmer Avenue. The proposed parking area is 20' by 40' with 5 spaces. UDO Section 20.04.060(i)(2)(B)(ii) limits alley-accessed parking areas to 20' by 20', so the petitioner is requesting a variance to allow the larger area, plus a second variance to raise the maximum permitted parking spaces for a duplex from 4 to 5.

The petitioner proposes removing the existing residence and building a new 2-story duplex with two 3-bedroom, 3-bath units, each with a separate exterior entrance facing E. Wylie Street to the north. The site plan shows 44.5% impervious surface coverage, within the 45% maximum allowed in the R3 district.

The duplex is set back at the required 15' front build-to line along both E. Wylie Street and the S. Palmer Avenue right-of-way, with a compliant 10' east side setback and 25' rear setback. Per the City's Transportation Plan, S. Palmer Avenue is intended as a future multiuse trail, so a driveway through it is not permitted. Since the alley to the south is currently unimproved, the petitioner has the option of accessing the property via E. Wylie Street or improving the alley, and has chosen the latter on the revised site plan.

The City's Senior Environmental Planner conducted a site visit and found no closed canopy present, so UDO Section 20.04.030(h) does not apply. Street trees are required, and shown as compliant, along both E. Wylie Street and S. Palmer Avenue. The petition was presented to a joint meeting of the Bryan Park and Elm Heights Neighborhood Associations on May 7, 2026; in response to feedback there, the petitioner added a proposed 6' tall opaque privacy fence along the east side of the lot.

Use-specific standards — UDO 20.03.030(b)(3)

Staff reviewed the standards applicable to a "Dwelling, Duplex" use and found the proposal consistent with each:

  • (A)(i) Notices of Violation: the property owner had none on file with Planning and Transportation at the time of application.
  • (B) Occupancy: subject to the UDO's definition of "Family" — in R3, up to three unrelated adults and their dependent children per unit.
  • (C)(i) Separate entrances: each unit has its own exterior entrance, both facing E. Wylie Street.
  • (C)(ii) Design compatibility with the block face (S. Dunn Street to S. Grant Street), across four required elements:
    1. Roof pitch: the revised one-story-appearing elevation still runs its roofline east/west, matching the block face.
    2. Front porch width and depth: typical relative to the mix of awnings, porches, and uncovered entries already present on the block.
    3. Front building setback: built at the required 15' build-to line, matching the house to the west and the current structure's location; older homes set farther back predate current UDO standards.
    4. Vehicle parking access: revised to remove the E. Wylie Street drivecut in favor of alley access — the specific arrangement is the subject of the variance requests below.
  • (C)(iii) Bedroom count: six bedrooms total, at the maximum allowed.
  • (D) 150' buffer: not within 150 feet of another approved duplex.
  • (E) Annual maximum: the BZA has approved three conditional-use duplexes in 2026 to date, under the 15-per-year cap.

Criteria and findings for Conditional Use permit

General compliance criteria — UDO 20.06.040(d)(6)(B)

Every petition is reviewed against: compliance with the UDO, with other applicable regulations, with utility/service/improvement standards, and with prior approvals.

Proposed finding: the petition meets the applicable use-specific standards described above. There are no Notices of Violation on file, each unit has a separate entrance, the four design elements match the block face, bedroom count and the 150' buffer requirement are both met, and the required neighborhood meeting was held on May 7, 2026. No other applicable regulations are known to apply. Water and sanitary service will be coordinated with City of Bloomington Utilities and electrical service with Duke Energy; the City's systems have adequate capacity, and there are no known prior land-use approvals for this site.

Additional criteria applicable to Conditional Uses — UDO 20.06.040(d)(6)(C)

i. Consistency with the Comprehensive Plan and other applicable plans

Proposed finding: the site's Future Land Use Map designation is "Mixed Urban Residential" — older neighborhoods built on a traditional block-and-grid street pattern. Comprehensive Plan Policy 5.3.1 encourages infill and redevelopment with increased residential density and underutilized housing types such as duplexes; this proposal advances that goal.

ii. Adequate public services and facilities

Proposed finding: City of Bloomington Utilities confirms adequate water and sewer capacity. New concrete walkways connect the duplex to E. Wylie Street as required by UDO Section 20.04.050(d)(4)(C). The revised parking arrangement removes the E. Wylie Street drivecut in favor of alley access from the improved stub of S. Palmer Avenue.

iii. Minimizes or mitigates adverse impacts

Proposed finding: no regulated natural or scenic features are affected; the site has no closed canopy and is not a historic site. No significant adverse impacts or changes to trash/waste service are expected. Following the May 7, 2026 neighborhood meeting, the petitioner added a proposed 6' opaque privacy fence along the east property line to buffer the adjacent use — carried forward as a condition of approval.

iv. Rational phasing plan

Proposed finding: no phasing is proposed.

Criteria and findings for the development standards variances

A variance may be approved only on a written finding that each of the following three criteria is met.

1. Not injurious to public health, safety, morals, and general welfare

Maximum vehicle parking: not expected to be injurious — one additional space beyond the otherwise-compliant design would have no community-wide impact.

Vehicle parking location and design: is expected to be injurious — the request doubles the size UDO 20.04.060(i)(2)(B)(ii) permits for alley-accessed parking, and a wider private/public interface increases safety risk for anyone using the alley on foot.

2. No substantially adverse effect on adjacent use and value

Maximum vehicle parking: no adverse impacts found — one additional compliant space would not affect surrounding property use or value.

Vehicle parking location and design: adverse impacts are found — the size limit exists to manage vehicular access and prevent alley-traffic blockage; a 40-foot drivecut serving five vehicles is not comparable to surrounding access design and increases pedestrian and vehicular risk if the alley is later extended east.

3. Practical difficulties peculiar to the property that the variance would relieve

Maximum vehicle parking: no practical difficulties found — the property can be developed with a compliant 4-space maximum.

Vehicle parking location and design: no practical difficulties found — since the alley is currently unimproved, the petitioner has the option of a compliant driveway off E. Wylie Street or a compliant 20'-by-20' parking pad via the alley; nothing peculiar to this property prevents meeting the standard either way.

Recommendation

Staff recommends the BZA:

Adopt the proposed findings, approve the Conditional Use request, and deny both variance requests, subject to the following conditions:

  1. The conditional use approval is limited to the design shown and discussed in the packet.
  2. A right-of-way use permit is required prior to any work in the public right-of-way.
  3. Per the submitted site plan, a 6' tall opaque fence is required along the east property line.
