  2. ZR2026-03-0010 — 1017 W. 9th Street

    Variance from tree preservation standards for a single-family detached dwelling in the Residential Small-Lot (R3) zoning district, by Zando Pleimann & Fiona Taggart.

    Case manager: Jamie Kreindler. Presented by Eric Greulich.

    Staff recommended denying the requested variance.

    Zando Pleimann and Fiona Taggart (petitioners) presented.

    Public comment

    • Kari Bennett
    • Joan Middendorf

    Motion: Fernandez moved to adopt Amended Findings of Fact and approve the petition; Kutsenkow seconded.

    Passed 3:0 (roll call), with an added condition: petitioner shall work with staff on a remediation plan including a minimum of 3 large canopy tree species.

    Amended findings of fact

    1. Public health, safety, and welfare: the variance is not expected to be injurious, given the applicant's committed tree replacement plan maintaining canopy coverage, consistent with the Comprehensive Plan and Climate Action Plan.
    2. Adjacent use and value: the tree replacement plan addresses canopy loss and is consistent with the City's adopted tree canopy policies.
    3. Practical difficulties: the site's constraints make the minimum requirement of nine large canopy trees infeasible; the variance represents the minimum relief necessary for practical, functional use of the property.

  3. ZR2026-03-0011 — 250 S. Pete Ellis Drive

    Variance from maximum drive width and maximum parking standards to allow construction of a "vehicle repair, minor" structure in the Mixed Use Corridor (MC) zoning district, by Valvoline.

    Case manager: Gabriel Holbrow. Presented by Eric Greulich.

    Staff recommended denying the drive-width variance and approving the parking-allowance variance, with conditions including a maximum of six onsite spaces plus one van-accessible space, and specific paving/pervious-surface requirements.

    Jonathan Wocher (consultant) presented on behalf of the petitioner.

    Public comment

    • David Kamen
    • Tom Orman
    • Brian Waltz
    • Dave Harstad

    Motion: Fernandez moved to adopt Amended Findings of Fact and approve, with amended conditions allowing up to eight onsite spaces plus one van-accessible space (nine total), with specified paving and pervious-surface requirements. Kutsenkow seconded.

    Passed 3:0 (roll call).

    Flavia Burrell separately moved to suspend the rules to end the meeting at 9:00 p.m.; passed by voice vote, 3:0.

    Amended findings of fact

    1. Adjacent use and value (parking number): up to eight spaces plus one van-accessible space will not be injurious, so long as spaces exceeding the UDO maximum use approved pervious surfaces; more than seven total hard-surfaced spaces would increase localized heat and reduce stormwater filtration while encouraging vehicle over-dependence.
    2. Adjacent use and value: up to eight spaces plus one van-accessible space will not substantially affect adjacent property use or value.
    3. Practical difficulties (drive width): strict compliance would impose unreasonable constraints on functional site design; the variance represents the minimum relief necessary for a workable layout.

  7. ZR2026-04-0017 — 2660 E. 2nd Street

    Variance from sign standards to allow an off-premise sign not on the tenant's space, for an indoor recreation use in the Mixed Use Corridor (MC) zoning district, by Leighla Taylor.

    Case manager: Jamie Kreindler. Presented by Eric Greulich.

    Staff recommended denying the petition.

    Leighla Taylor (petitioner) presented.

    Motion: Fernandez moved to adopt Amended Findings of Fact and approve; Kutsenkow seconded.

    Passed 3:0 (roll call), with an added condition: any sign must be consistent with the size, placement, and design depicted in the staff report.

    Amended finding of fact #3

    Practical difficulties: strict application of the UDO would create practical difficulties given the building's unique configuration and the tenant's lease space, limiting adequate sign visibility. The existing 32-square-foot sign on the building's south side and the new freestanding-sign panel fronting E. 2nd Street do not provide sufficient visibility for the tenant businesses given the property's specific design and conditions.
